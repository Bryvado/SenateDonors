      About this map

      Itemized contributions from individuals to the principal federal campaign committees of James Talarico, John Cornyn, and Ken Paxton, placed at each contributor's reported ZIP. Receipt dates define the three periods (through the Mar 3 primary, Mar 4 – May 26 runoff, and since May 27); drag the two Period handles to include any continuous stretch of them. Amendments replace superseded reports. Unitemized contributions (generally $200 or less per donor per election) are not included.

      Who led tints every place toward whichever of the two compared candidates raised more there, in four steps per side by the leader's share (50–55%, 55–65%, 65–80%, 80%+); even a narrow lead is shown, and only an exact tie is neutral. There is no fixed cutoff for places with few dollars: colors fade smoothly toward pale as the dollars behind them shrink, relative to the places shown. Total raised is the pair's combined dollars. Per 100 residents divides that by ACS 2020–2024 population and fades places with few residents, whose rates are less stable. For these two, each color holds about a fifth of the places shown, and the legend lists the cutoffs. Only one level is colored at a time: states when nothing is open, otherwise just the open areas, with other states left plain. Contribution counts are entries, not unique donors.

      ZIP outlines are 2020 Census ZIP Code Tabulation Areas (ZCTAs), which do not match every mailing ZIP. The County, Congressional district, Metro/micro area, and County subdivision views apportion each reported ZIP's totals among the areas it overlaps, using the share of the ZIP's residential addresses in each area (all addresses for ZIPs with none) from the HUD USPS ZIP crosswalk (June 2026). Amounts and counts there are estimates. Areas where neither compared candidate has receipts in the selected period are not drawn. ZIPs outside the crosswalk and non-metro or unassigned portions are left out of the shading, but state totals still include them. Congressional districts are the 119th Congress districts, which the HUD crosswalk uses; Texas's 2025 mid-decade plan is not shown.
